# Project overview

When Nature Strikes is an undergraduate Computer Science Engineering project
about natural-disaster frequency and reported harm. Its primary research
question is whether the most frequent natural disasters are also the most
destructive. The project compares counts, accumulated losses, and the average
and median loss among reporting events.

Floods lead frequency and total affected population in the full snapshot.
The leaders for economic damage and mortality differ. The project treats
reporting frequency, calendar coverage and hazard composition as part of
interpretation. A secondary analysis tests how far simple metadata can rank
reported high-mortality events in a chronological holdout. The profile study
investigates structure in observed human and economic impacts.
