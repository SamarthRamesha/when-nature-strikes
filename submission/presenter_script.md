# Presenter script

Allow roughly 11 minutes for 18 slides and two minutes for the dashboard.
The editable presentation also includes speaker notes.

| Slide | Speaking cue |
| --- | --- |
| 1 | Introduce the team and the project title. Ask the audience to name a frequent hazard and a disaster remembered for its losses. |
| 2 | State the research question. Separate how often a hazard occurs from how much damage it accumulates and what a typical reporting event looks like. |
| 3 | Describe one cross-border event with two affected countries. Explain why it contributes one global count and why each reporting country contribution remains in the loss sum. |
| 4 | Show the five leading hazards. Flood accounts for 5,814 of 15,727 events. Several hazards have only one record, which makes some ranks unstable. |
| 5 | Compare flooded populations, storm damage and earthquake mean damage. Show the historical drought result and the singleton fog mortality median. |
| 6 | Read one Spearman interval from the axis. Explain the built-in count component of totals and the weaker relationship for per-event summaries. |
| 7 | Explain the open marker at 2026 and the historical coverage problem. A change in recording can shift the chart without a change in physical hazard incidence. |
| 8 | Define the positive label of at least 100 reported deaths in a single-country event. Describe the time split and show the three event metadata inputs. |
| 9 | Compare AP 0.288 with the 0.116 prior baseline. The chosen threshold detected 34 of 97 positives. A useful historical signal can coexist with inadequate recall. |
| 10 | Show hazard as the top global group and explain the log-odds waterfall from baseline to the fitted score. Attribute the patterned scores to the model and background. |
| 11 | Compare the held-out permutation result with SHAP. Discuss LIME fidelity and show the month response summary. |
| 12 | Explain the fully reporting 1,821-event cohort. PCA summarizes variance. The two nonlinear methods emphasize local neighbor relationships that change with parameters. |
| 13 | K-means identified a moderate severity gradient. DBSCAN yielded one density-connected group plus noise. Show the top 19 anomaly flags as records to review. |
| 14 | Switch to the live dashboard or use the genuine overview capture. Keep a flood filter active as you change views and compare the three metrics. |
| 15 | Show how the map matches ISO codes. Point out the unmatched table and caution against comparing per-capita risk from unnormalized counts. |
| 16 | Run the guided story and open the native Power BI project on Windows. Refresh and show the saved PBIX workflow. |
| 17 | Return to the research question. Flood leads frequency and accumulated affected population, and other hazards lead other definitions. A reporting denominator belongs beside each loss. |
| 18 | Name EM-DAT, the missingness work and the methods references. Identify prior team work and Codex assistance using the contribution matrix. Invite questions. |
